A new RMS version event (payment received, extras added, cabin changed) supersedes the previous send and re-queues delivery automatically. Nothing is attached by hand.

Every contact is enrolled in at most one journey per goal. Journeys exit on conversion (never keep nurturing someone who booked), suppress on unsubscribe, and hand off to a person when a human touch beats another email. Payment and pre-departure journeys are transactional (no opt-in needed); nurture and re-engagement require marketing consent. Every enrolment and exit is driven by an event from the engine or the RMS — the trigger column names the exact event.

Segments recompute continuously from five dimensions — behaviour (engine + email events), interest (declared + inferred), location, profile (party type, LTV from the RMS ledger), promotion (exposure and response to offers published in the RMS). A contact can sit in several segments; journey enrolment resolves by priority. Segments push to Meta/Google remarketing audiences and back to the booking engine for on-site personalisation.

Offers are created and published in the RMS (Rates & Promotions) and rendered by the booking engine. The CRM does not invent discounts — it mirrors the live offer, attaches the audience and the creative, and measures what each campaign produced against the RMS ledger. Redemption is counted from bookings that carry the offer code, so campaign ROI and revenue never diverge from the accounts.

One rule resolves conflicts: if a booking carries a partner ID, the trade attribution wins over the marketing last-touch for commission purposes; marketing keeps the touch for media reporting. Both are stored — neither overwrites the other.

The booking engine emits a behavioural event for every meaningful action. The CRM consumes them to build the contact timeline, score intent, drive segments and fire recovery journeys; the RMS consumes only the two that change inventory (hold placed, request submitted). Anonymous events are stitched to a contact the moment an email is captured — the whole prior session is back-filled onto the timeline.

Internal alerts are the human escape hatch from automation. Each has a named recipient, a response SLA and an acknowledgement — an unacknowledged alert escalates one level. Alerts are raised by RMS events (payment, commission, occupancy, NPS) and by CRM events (SLA breach, high-value lead), and every one of them also lands as a task in the queue.

This is the contract between the three systems. One field, one owner. The owner is the only system that may write it; every other system holds a read-only mirror kept current by events. There is no two-way field sync anywhere in this design, so there is no last-writer-wins conflict to resolve. Delivery is at-least-once with an event_id; consumers dedupe on it.
